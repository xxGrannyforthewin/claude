- **The margin:** each in-store order (about $85 average) leaves **$42.09 (50%)** after the cost of the clothes, card fees and the bag. The store costs about **$11,820 a month** to keep open (rent $4,375, about $6,290 of estimated wages, utilities and the rest).
- **Break-even: about 9.4 orders a day, call it 10. That's about 280 orders, or about $24,000 of store sales, every month.** That's more than your whole online business did in a month ($22.2K in 60 days).
- **The line to watch: daily orders (the ramp).** The month-by-month ramp below is my guess, built around the Muslim calendar: a holiday bump in Nov/Dec, a dip in January, and peaks before Ramadan (from about Feb 8, 2027) and Eid al-Fitr (about Mar 9–10, 2027). The simulated panel says November is a slow month for these shoppers. At an average of 8 orders a day the store loses about **$39,600 in year 1**. At 12 a day it makes about **$11,600**. The real numbers from your first two weeks will tell us which.
- **Cash:** the build-out is about $37,400 (most already paid). With the guessed ramp, losses on top of that bring the cash needed to about **$77,000** before the store pays for itself. That doesn't include the ~$40,000 of opening stock, which comes back as it sells.

**Three ways to improve the margin (each one run through the tool):**
1. **Sell more per visit.** At about $95 per order instead of $85 (add a $10 hijab, or a bisht/shawl bundle), break-even drops from about 10 to **8 a day**, and year 1 improves by about $24,000 (to -$15,300).
2. **Match staffing to traffic.** Two paid people instead of three (the owner covers slow weekday hours) also brings break-even to **8 a day**, and year 1 improves by about $25,000 (to -$14,400).
3. **Bring more people in.** 25% more orders than the guessed ramp roughly halves the loss (to about -$14,000). 50% more turns the store profitable (+$11,600).
   Doing #1 and #2 together is the cheapest path to a store that pays for itself.
