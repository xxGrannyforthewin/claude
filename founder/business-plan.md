## The idea

- What it is: Bonita Style's first physical store, a "Modest Boutique" at 8401 Westheimer Rd, Suite 130, Houston, TX 77063, selling embroidered thobes, kaftans, abayas, jalabiyas, bishts and coordinated sets. Grand opening Sunday, November 1, 2026, 10 AM. The brand already sells online (Shopify, TikTok Shop, Etsy, Amazon, Walmart) and has about 30,000 TikTok followers.
- Who it is for: women who dress modestly and want pieces that look special without feeling costume-like, shopping for Eid and Ramadan, weddings and henna nights, family gatherings, gifting and everyday wear. In Houston, especially women who want to try pieces on before buying. Their objections: "will it fit me?", "is the fabric see-through or scratchy?", "does the color match the photo?"
- What it sells, at what price: core thobes $74.99–$79.99 (Mira, Lujain, Luna, Marwa, Zina, Israa); Sorrah Abaya $45; Georgette Chiffon Hijab $10 add-on; Layla Bisht $94.99; Wid Jalabiya Set $119.99; Noosa Thobe $179.99. Opening-day offer announced in content: 15% off everything, Nov 1 only.
- Where and how: walk-in boutique on Westheimer Rd (Westchase/Sharpstown area, southwest Houston), found through TikTok/Instagram/Facebook content, TikTok Lives from the store, the Google Maps listing and an RSVP page for the opening. The store also packs online orders.
- Budget and constraints (from the owner's cashflow sheet and checklist):
  - Rent $4,375/month; utilities about $345; phone $50; cameras $10 (store monthly about $4,780).
  - Build-out about $37,400 planned (deposit + first rent $9,000 paid, flooring $6,000 paid, painting $2,000 paid, decor stands $3,500 paid, sign $10,000, chandeliers $1,500, misc decor $2,000, packaging $1,000, plumbing/electrical $836, POS $350, cameras $1,000, occupancy permit $200).
  - Inventory for the store: about $40,000 for about 500 pieces (as written on the sheet).
  - Online business reference: last 60 days 240 orders, about $22.2K net sales (avg order about $92). August plan: $19,500 sales, $9,000 COGS (about 46%), about $2,223/month in software + ads (TikTok ads $2,000).
  - Staff: three hourly roles (including a Content Creator employee; "Amy" on the team), training Oct 26–Nov 1. Hourly pay not yet recorded.
  - Team: Abdullah (owner) and family; entity Bonita Style LLC (Texas LLC being merged into the California LLC).
  - Already decided: the lease is signed and the store opens Nov 1. The question is not "should we open" but "how do we make this store profitable and fill it".

## Summary

**Bonita Style Houston** is the first physical store of an online modest-fashion brand (about 30K TikTok followers, about $22K online sales in 60 days). It sells embroidered crepe thobes ($74.99–$79.99), everyday abayas ($45), sets and hijabs to modest women in southwest Houston. It opens Sunday, November 1, 2026 at 8401 Westheimer Rd, Suite 130.

**Verdict: Not yet.** Two of the plan's checks fail:
- **Year 1 is a projected loss of about $39,600.** This uses an estimated ramp averaging about 6–7 orders a day against a break-even of about **10 orders a day (~$24K store sales a month)**.
- **The simulated panel buys at 20%**, below the pack's default 25% bar. It was 15% before the offer was improved.

Each order does earn money (**$42.09, 50%**), so the problem is volume and fixed costs, not the price.

**What has to change, and which skill changes it:**
1. **Lower the break-even** (CFO). A $95 average order (hijab add-on, bisht bundle) brings break-even to about 9 a day (+$12.5K in year 1). 2 paid staff instead of 3 brings it to 8 (+$25K). **Both together: 7 a day, and year 1 is close to even (about -$1,900)** on the same cautious ramp.
2. **Fix the calendar** (marketing). November is off-season for most of these shoppers. Turn "not yet" into the **Ramadan early-access list**, and use wedding season. Ramadan starts around Feb 8 and Eid al-Fitr is around Mar 9–10, 2027.
3. **Replace the estimates with real numbers** (owner → CFO): the landed cost per piece and the real wages. If a piece really costs about $80 landed (as the inventory sheet implies), every sale loses money, and nothing else matters until that's fixed.

**Where the panel and the board agreed:** the fitting room is the reason the store should exist (fit, sheerness, colour). Every simulated buyer who bought did so for try-on and convenience. **Where they differed:** the board's Monopoly lens worries about copycats (Asli Modest Wear is 5.0 from 500+ reviews on Hwy 6 S). The panel's bigger "no"s were timing, price anchoring to $25 online abayas, and loyalty to existing Hillcroft shops.

**Biggest risk:** a slow November and December burns cash before the Ramadan peak. **What's being done:** a capture-the-list offer, staffing matched to traffic, and a day-14 and day-30 review with real numbers.

**To start:** the build-out (about $37,400, mostly paid) plus up to about $40,000 of operating losses in year 1 on the current estimates. The ~$40K of stock comes back as it sells. **First real test:** opening week against the success line in launch.md (40 orders on day one, 6+ a day after, 100 list sign-ups, 15 reviews by Nov 14).

**This week:** get the **Texas sales tax permit and the Certificate of Occupancy** sorted (both show as not done on your sheet), and **pull the supplier invoice + freight bill to get the true landed cost per piece** before Nov 1.

## What the board said

**Vote: 3 FUND IF, 0 pass. Average score 5.0/10** (Offers 5, Monopoly 4, Product 6).

The lenses are summaries of published frameworks. The board members are not those authors and do not speak for them.

### Risks more than one member raised (most serious first)

1. **Nobody knows the true cost per piece** (all three). The cashflow sheet says $40,000 for 500 pieces, about $80 each. The retail price is $75–80, and the online plan implies about 46% cost of goods. Both can't be true. If $80 is right, every sale loses money and 15% off makes it worse.
2. **The blanket "15% off everything" opening offer** (Offers, Monopoly, Product). It is a price move with no proof the margin can carry it.
3. **Nobody knows where customers come from** (Offers, Monopoly). 234 of 240 online orders have no referrer. Nobody knows how many TikTok followers live in Houston, or what one store customer costs to bring in.
4. **The Houston competitors are unmapped** (Monopoly, Offers). The Istanbul supplier is open to anyone, so a nearby shop could sell the same look.
5. **The store must sell enough to cover its costs** (Offers, Monopoly). Rent and running costs are about $4,780 a month before wages. At about $40–42 gross profit a piece, the store must sell about 115 pieces a month just to cover the space.

### Where they disagreed

- **Product (6)** sees the fitting room as a real product advantage, and thinks the danger is running it badly: no focus, a crowded floor, staff trained in six days.
- **Monopoly (4)** sees nothing that stops copycats, and wants exclusive or own-design pieces and a customer list as the long-term moat.
- **Offers (5)** thinks the fix is the offer itself: a stack, a guarantee and bundles instead of a discount.

The strongest version is not "the website, in a shop". ### 5. The gap

**Local try-on of embroidered, occasion-ready pieces in the $45–$80 band, with honest information on size, opacity and color.**

The evidence:
- **Nobody local is clearly in this band.** Hayat's tops out around $65. Asli's occasion pieces are $90–$199. The others have no published prices. Online shoppers who want to spend less get $22–$59 pieces, and the top complaints about those are sheer fabric, photo mismatch and sizing (themes 1 and 2, 11 excerpts). Those are exactly the three objections in Bonita's own buyer profile.
- **Online delivery fails at the worst moment.** Theme 3 includes a cancelled $600 Eid order. A store where you can try a piece on and take it home the same day solves the pre-Eid deadline that online brands miss.
- **Sizing is a repeated complaint.** One-size-fits-all at Amal, oversized at Junaid Jamshed. A boutique that stocks petite through plus sizes, and says so, addresses a documented complaint.
- **Service is not a gap against Asli.** Asli has a 5.0 Google rating from 500+ reviews. The gap is price and placement: Bonita sits between Hayat's and Asli, and in a different part of southwest Houston (Westheimer rather than Hwy 6 S or Beechnut). It is not a "better service" story.

**Caveat:** there may be more stores on Google Maps that this research could not read. Confirm by walking the Hillcroft and Westheimer corridors before relying on "nobody local".

### 6. The threat

**Asli Modest Wear is the most likely to copy Bonita fastest.**
- It is the best-reviewed local store (5.0, 500+ Google reviews).
- It already carries abayas, kaftans and thobes.
- It sells online.
- It could add a $75–$80 embroidered line, or run sales on its $89.99 abayas, within one season.

**Hayat's Fashion is the price threat.** Its $40–$65 abayas sit at or below Bonita's $45 Sorrah Abaya, also in southwest Houston (Beechnut, 77083).

**Online marketplaces (Amazon, SHEIN, TikTok Shop) are the structural threat.** Copycat embroidered abayas can be listed in days at $35–$50. Bonita's own TikTok audience is already exposed to these sellers. The defence is what an online listing cannot do: try-on, same-day pickup before Eid, and in-person fit help.
