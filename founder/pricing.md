# Pricing: Bonita Style Houston

Sources: the 20-buyer panel's price answers (`pricing-curve.md`), the competitor map (`competitors.md`) and the CFO model (`cfo.md`). Simulated buyers help pick what to test. They don't prove anything.

## 1. The price: keep the thobes at $74.99–$79.99. Don't discount them after opening day.

- **Panel:** acceptable range **$30–$95**. The median buyer starts calling it "getting expensive" at $75 and "too expensive" at $110. So $75–80 sits at the top of comfortable, but inside the range.
- **Competitors:** the closest local stores are **Hayat's Fashion** (Beechnut, abayas from $40, reversible $65) and **Asli Modest Wear** (Hwy 6 S, abayas $89.99–$119.99, kaftans $169.99–$199). Bonita's thobes sit **in the gap between them**. No local store clearly owns $45–$80 embroidered occasion wear (unverified on Google Maps: walk Hillcroft and Westheimer to confirm).
- **Margin:** at the current mix (~$85 per order), each order leaves **$42.09**. Dropping the price 10% raises break-even from about 10 to 12 orders a day (CFO what-if). Raising the average order to $95 (cost of goods still 46%) lowers it to about 9 (8.3), or to 7 if weekday staffing also drops to 2 paid people. **The money is in a bigger basket, not a lower price.**

## 2. The ladder (good / better / best)

| rung | what | price | why step up |

## 3. The opening offer

- **Nov 1 only: 15% off everything.** It's already announced publicly, so keep the promise. At ~46% cost of goods, a $78 thobe still leaves about $30. *If the landed cost turns out to be about $80 a piece, stop and rethink.*
- **Nov 2–8: free matching hijab with any thobe** (a gift, not a discount, so it doesn't train buyers to wait for sales).
- **No "was $X" prices** on anything that was never sold at that price.

## 4. What to test with real buyers (first 3 weeks)

1. **Everyday abaya bundle: 2 for $80 vs 2 for $75.** Run each for one week at the register and on TikTok Lives. Compare units per day and contribution per bundle. (In the re-test, one deal-hunter said she'd come in at "2 abayas for $60". That's below what the margin supports, so don't go there.)
2. **Hijab add-on rate:** how many thobe buyers take a $10 hijab when it's offered in the fitting room vs only at the register. Target: 1 in 3.
3. **Basket size:** track average order value weekly. At $95, break-even falls to about 9 orders a day (7 with 2 paid staff on weekdays).

## 5. The panel's price objections (verbatim, for marketing to answer)

- "I get my abayas for $20 to $30 on Amazon or TikTok Shop and wait for sales, so $45 for an everyday one, or $75 to $80 for a thobe, is more than I usually pay, even with 15% off." (P003)
- "Eid is months away and I don't have a wedding coming up, so I can't justify $75 to $80 for a thobe when I can get an abaya for $25 online." (P005)
- "My sister in Karachi and my cousins in Dubai send me pieces … where an embroidered thobe costs me a fraction of $75." (P010)

The answer to all three isn't a lower price. It's **showing the fabric up close next to a cheap online abaya**, plus "you can take it home fitted, the same day, before the event". Several panel buyers named that as what would change their mind.
