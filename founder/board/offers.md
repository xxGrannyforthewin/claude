# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The market has money and is easy to reach.** Buyers spend on occasions they care about (Eid, weddings, henna nights) and buy for gifts. The brand reaches them already: about 30,000 TikTok followers and about 240 online orders in 60 days at an average of about $92.
- **The store raises likelihood, which is where offers usually fail.** The three objections in the brief ("will it fit?", "is it see-through or scratchy?", "does the color match?") are all doubts about whether the outcome will happen. Trying the piece on answers all three. Online, nothing can do that.
- **Time to the outcome is short.** A buyer walks out with the outfit the same day, with no shipping wait before an event.
- **The dream outcome is clear:** looking special at a family moment without looking like a costume.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The offer competes on price, and the margin is unknown.** The opening offer is "15% off everything". The lens treats that as a commodity move. The inventory sheet works out to about $80 a piece against a retail price of $75–80. If that is the real landed cost, every discounted sale loses money. Even at the online plan's ~46% cost of goods, gross profit on a $77 thobe falls from about $42 to about $30 at 15% off.
2. **There is no stack, guarantee or name.** The store sells the same pieces as the website, only in person. Nothing makes a buyer feel stupid saying no. Nothing turns the five overstocked thobe styles (about 430 units) or the high-priced dead stock (Noosa, Layla Bisht, Wid set) into a reason to buy today.
3. **The volume needed to break even is unproven.** The store costs about $4,780 a month before wages. At about $42 gross profit a piece, that is about 115 pieces a month just to cover the store. Wages, local foot traffic and Houston competitors are all unknown. With 234 of 240 online orders untracked, nobody can yet tell which content brings people in.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the true landed cost per piece, including freight and duty from Istanbul? Does the $40,000 for 500 pieces include the fixtures or the online stock?
- What are the three hourly wages, and how many pieces a month does the store need to sell to cover rent plus staff?
- Which Houston stores sell abayas and thobes, and at what prices?
- What could the store offer that costs little and is worth a lot? For example: an occasion styling session, a matching hijab included, steaming and gift-wrapping, a fit-exchange guarantee.
- Could a "complete Eid / wedding look" sell at a higher price than the single piece and still be the obvious choice?
- What real deadline is coming: Black Friday (Nov 27), the winter wedding season, Ramadan in early 2027?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Know the cost before discounting.** Confirm the landed cost per piece before Nov 1. If the margin cannot carry 15%, drop the blanket discount.
2. **Swap the discount for a stack.** For example: buy any thobe on opening day and get the matching hijab ($10 value) plus a styling session, or a gift with purchase while stock lasts. Keep the cash price whole.
3. **Add a guarantee that fits the store**, such as a fit or color exchange within a set number of days. Have the CFO set the terms.
4. **Use dead stock as bonuses and bundles** (the bisht in a wedding-party bundle, the Misk Shawl as a gift), not as markdowns.
5. **Track where buyers come from from day one.** Ask "how did you hear about us?" at the register. Use RSVP and Lives codes. Make the monthly break-even piece count the single number the team watches.
