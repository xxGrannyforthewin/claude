# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market it could own.** That market is "try it on before Eid or a wedding" modest wear in southwest Houston. The buyer's three objections (fit, sheerness, colour match) are exactly what a physical store answers and online sellers cannot. Starting narrow is the right shape.
- **It already has distribution.** There are about 30K TikTok followers, a working online business (240 orders, about $22.2K in 60 days) and live selling. Most new boutiques open with none of this.
- **The start of a brand.** Named pieces, embroidered designs and content made from the store are the only moat this lens sees within reach for a small retailer.
- **Data competitors lack.** Sixty days of sell-through tells it what moves (Baydaa, Israa, Lujain) before buying for the store.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nothing stops a copycat.** The pieces come from an Istanbul supplier others can reach too. If a nearby boutique stocks similar thobes at similar prices, this becomes a crowded market with thin margins. The Houston competitors are an unanswered question, and the business may be opening into a fight it has not mapped.
2. **The online reach may not be local.** We do not know how many of the 30K followers live in Houston. 234 of 240 online orders have no referrer, so the brand cannot see which channel brings buyers. So we cannot answer the lens's key question, how the first 100 store customers arrive and what each costs.
3. **The margin may not exist.** The sheet implies about $80 landed per piece against a $75–80 retail price. The online plan implies cost of goods around 46%. Those two cannot both be true. If 46% is right, each core thobe leaves roughly $40 after cost of goods. Rent and running costs alone (about $4,780 a month) would then need about 115 pieces a month before wages, which are unknown. If $80 is right, every sale loses money, and 15% off on opening day makes it worse.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the true landed cost per piece, including freight and duties?
- Who else sells abayas and thobes within a 20-minute drive of Westheimer, and at what prices?
- What share of your TikTok audience is in Houston?
- What do you sell that a local customer cannot get from the same Turkish suppliers through someone else? Exclusive designs, your own embroidery, custom fitting?
- How will you know which channel brought each store customer? A code, a question at the till, an RSVP match?
- What is the plan for the $179.99 Noosa, the Layla Bisht and the Wid sets, which are not selling?
- In ten years, why is this the store Houston's modest shoppers think of first?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Before Nov 1:** confirm the landed cost per piece. Stop any discount that pushes a sale below cost.
2. **Within 30 days:** map the local competitors and their prices. Name one thing the store owns that they do not (exclusive or own-design pieces, fitting and alterations, Eid pre-order). Then say so in every piece of content.
3. **From day one:** record how each store buyer found you (POS question or code). After 60 days, report the cost per store customer by channel.
4. **Build toward a moat:** own-label or exclusive designs from the supplier, plus a customer list (WhatsApp/SMS) captured at the till, so repeat buyers and community events like Eid and henna-night styling become the defence.
